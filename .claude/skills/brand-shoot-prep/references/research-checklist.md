# Deep Research Checklist

Goal: enough understanding of the client to make confident creative and styling decisions.
Cite a source (URL) for non-obvious claims in `research.md`.

## Identity & positioning
- [ ] Full name, role/title, company, location
- [ ] What they sell / do, and to whom
- [ ] Positioning: premium vs. accessible, corporate vs. creative, established vs. challenger
- [ ] One-line value proposition in their own words (quote it)

## Audience
- [ ] Primary audience (clients, investors, recruiters, followers, press)
- [ ] What that audience needs to feel/believe when they see the photos
- [ ] Where images will be used (website hero, LinkedIn, press kit, speaking, social, ads)

## Brand voice & verbal tone
- [ ] Adjectives the brand/person uses about itself
- [ ] Formal vs. casual, serious vs. playful, technical vs. human

## Current visual identity
- [ ] Logo, brand colors (capture hex if visible)
- [ ] Typography (serif/sans, weight, character)
- [ ] Existing photography: style, lighting, backgrounds, crops, editing/color grade
- [ ] What's working / what looks dated or off-brand

## Competitive & category landscape
- [ ] 2–4 direct competitors or peers
- [ ] How they shoot their people/brand (the visual norms of the category)
- [ ] An opportunity to differentiate visually

## Signals & recency
- [ ] Recent posts, talks, launches, press, awards
- [ ] Any upcoming context (book, funding, rebrand) the shoot should anticipate

## Synthesis (write into research.md)
- [ ] Who they are — 2–3 sentences
- [ ] Audience + intended use
- [ ] Brand voice
- [ ] Current visual identity (palette / fonts / photo style)
- [ ] Competitor visual landscape + the gap to exploit
- [ ] **3–5 visual keywords** to steer the boards (e.g. "warm, editorial, candid, earthy, architectural")
